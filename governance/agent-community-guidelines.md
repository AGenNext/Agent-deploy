# Agent-Community Guidelines

**Status:** DRAFT · owned by `Agent-deploy`.

> Community agents — third-party, not ours — will also write and contribute. **We
> cannot force them to obey our internal protocol.** So we publish **guidelines**
> they choose to follow, and we **govern the gate, not the process.**

---

## The core honesty

You **cannot control how an outside agent works.** You can only control **what you
accept.**

- For **our** agents → the development & coding protocols are **enforced.**
- For **community** agents → these are **guidelines** (voluntary), and the **gate**
  validates their *output* at the boundary before it is accepted. Their *process*
  is theirs; their *result* must pass.

> Govern the gate, not the process. The gate is not a wall — it **secures,
> governs, enables, and educates** what passes.

---

## Anchored to open-source practice

These guidelines do not invent a new way to collaborate — they **anchor to proven
open-source community practice**, the way millions already work:

- **`CONTRIBUTING.md` / `CODE_OF_CONDUCT.md`** — clear contribution rules and norms;
- **fork → branch → pull request → review → merge** — the standard flow;
- **issues, discussions, RFCs** — propose before you build;
- **maintainers / reviewers** — the human (and agent) **third** that approves;
- **tests + CI required** — the gate; no merge on red;
- **small, focused PRs** — one step at a time (now → next);
- **semantic versioning + changelogs** — defined, traceable change;
- **open license** — anyone may use and contribute.

The agent layer sits **on top** of these, not instead of them: defined
instructions and gate-validation add **machine-checkable meaning** to practices
the community already trusts. *Know what exists; build the next edge on it.*

And because it builds on what people **already know**, it is **easy to explain and
easy to adopt** — no relearning, just the next edge on familiar ground.

---

## The guidelines (recommended)

We recommend the **Agent-Development** and **Agent-Coding** protocols:

- defined instructions, not prompts;
- one verified step at a time (now → next);
- ground every step — no inventing;
- run and test (the third is execution);
- no self-approval.

Following them makes a contribution **far more likely to pass the gate** — that is
the incentive, not coercion.

---

## What the gate enforces (regardless of process)

However a contribution was produced, the gate validates it before acceptance
(`Agent-deploy` validation, **no self-approval**):

- commit checks, release checks, **repo boundaries**;
- meaning conforms to the **canonical language** (one word, one meaning);
- outcomes **verified**; security, governance, compliance pass.

A contribution that **passes the gate is accepted**, whatever made it; one that
**fails is rejected**, however it was made.

---

## Balance & federation

- **Open** (anyone may contribute, any way) **+ the third** (the gate validates)
  **= balance.** Guidelines **invite**; the gate **protects.**
- **Federated:** each community or field may adopt or extend the guidelines for
  itself. Conformance is to the **open standard at the gate**, never to a mandated
  internal process.

> We educate and enable (guidelines), and secure and govern (the gate). The gate,
> not the wall — open to all, honest by the third.
