# The Three Layers

*Everything maps to three layers.*

The pattern recurs because of the root law — **two can act; only three can be
trusted.** So every complete thing has a **ground**, a **transform**, and a
**third** that makes it true.

---

## The three layers

1. **Understand** — the *ground*: what is true.
2. **Transform** — the *work*: what is done.
3. **Trust** — the *third*: what makes it believable.

---

## Everything maps to them

| framework | 1 · Understand (ground) | 2 · Transform (work) | 3 · Trust (the third) |
|---|---|---|---|
| **The Prestige** | Pledge | Turn | **Prestige** |
| **The three C's** | Context | Content | **Contract** |
| **Architecture** | Data | Code | **Contract** |
| **Process** | Define | Act | **Verify** |
| **Time** | Past | Present | **Future** |
| **Trust** | sign / party | act | **interpretant / verifier** |

Layer 1 grounds it. Layer 2 does the work. **Layer 3 — the third — is the one most
teams skip, and the one that turns a capability into something trusted.**

> Most ship layers 1 and 2. We ship **layer 3** — the third — in every loop.

---

## Honest note: the third isn't always last

Sometimes the binding third is the **end** — the *verifier* (Prestige, Verify).
Sometimes it is the **middle** — the *interpretant* that binds (Promise in
*propose → promise → provide*; Align in *define → align → act*; the contract that
gives a word its meaning).

Either position, the rule holds: the three are **incomplete without it.** Two can
act; only three can be trusted.

> *Strive for better — in a loop.*
